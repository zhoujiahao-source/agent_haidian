# Case 1 — Toronto / Vector Institute ecosystem

## Why selected

Toronto provides a useful research–talent–industry case centered around the Vector Institute and a wider university / technology ecosystem.

## Public evidence starting points

- Vector Institute official site: `https://vectorinstitute.ai/`
- Relevant university / public economic-development sources should be added only after current verification.

## Mechanism to study

- high-level AI research institution as ecosystem anchor;
- talent / university links;
- industry membership / applied transfer;
- public-interest / responsible-AI research as part of the ecosystem rather than an afterthought.

## Transfer lesson for Jing-Zhang

Do not interpret “world-class AI ecosystem” only as company density. The AI 原点社区 / 众智园 relationship should connect research, talent, applied validation and civic responsibility.

## What not to copy

- an institution-only model with weak public-space / community connection;
- brand prestige without a clear city capability-transfer pathway.

## Jing-Zhang design translation

`research / talent → capability validation → public-value test → capability registry / backbone transfer`

# Transfer synthesis for Jing-Zhang

The six cases suggest six questions the final ecosystem map should answer:

1. **Research:** Where is capability created?
2. **Talent:** Where do people learn, live, meet and move?
3. **Validation:** Where can a prototype be safely tested?
4. **Market / service:** Where does a capability encounter real demand?
5. **Support:** Where do capital, IP, professional services and governance enter?
6. **Public value:** Where is the result audited, shared, stopped, scaled or retired?

Jing-Zhang's differentiation is to make these functions **spatially distributed but exchangeable** through the Capability Backbone rather than concentrating all functions in a single AI campus.
