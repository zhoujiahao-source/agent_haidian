# Public Value Framework｜公共价值评审框架

## 1. Definition

For this proposal, **public value** means a meaningful improvement to a legitimate urban need that also passes common safety, equity, privacy, environmental, cost/dependency and human-control floors.

A model can be technically excellent and still fail public-value review. Conversely, stopping an unsafe or low-value experiment can be a successful governance outcome.

## 2. Common floors

### A. Safety
Review actual and plausible severe outcomes, near misses, safe-state behaviour, monitoring latency, recovery and physical/operational fallback. A serious unmitigated safety breach blocks SCALE.

### B. Rights & equity
Review distribution, access, exclusion, burden shifting, accessibility and differential error/benefit. Report average, subgroup and worst-case/tail evidence where meaningful. Improvement for the majority cannot erase serious deterioration for a vulnerable group.

### C. Privacy & data stewardship
Require purpose limitation, minimum necessary data, provenance, retention limits, access controls, notice/choice and impact assessment where appropriate. Sensitive or biometric data require elevated scrutiny.

### D. Environmental burden
Record incremental energy/material/resource burden and whether the AI-enabled intervention displaces environmental cost elsewhere. “Digital” is not treated as automatically low-impact.

### E. Cost and dependency
Measure public/operational cost, staffing, maintenance, switching cost, vendor lock-in, continuity risk and decommission cost. A cheap pilot that creates expensive irreversible dependence is not automatically high value.

### F. Human agency / exit
People and operators need an appropriate path to understand status, obtain human help, challenge consequential outcomes, opt out where required, override safely and exit/retire the capability.

## 3. Scenario-specific outcome layer

Each experiment adds one primary public outcome and supporting indicators, for example:

- heat-risk public space: exposure reduction / comfort / vulnerable-user access;
- navigation: successful accessible journey completion, not click-through rate;
- community service: resolved need and equitable access, not chatbot message count;
- emergency support: response quality and safe escalation, not automated-handling share;
- AI-native service: verified user/public benefit plus fair fallback, not adoption alone.

Metrics are selected before the pilot and cannot be retrofitted solely to produce a positive result.

## 4. Distribution rule

For each material outcome, report where feasible:

- overall result;
- relevant population/space subgroups;
- worst subgroup or worst quantile;
- missing/unknown coverage;
- complaint and override distribution;
- confidence and limitations.

No protected/sensitive attribute should be inferred merely to make a dashboard look complete. If equity cannot be validly measured, state the gap and use safer proxy/process review rather than fabricate precision.

## 5. Evidence confidence

Use:

- **high** — reproducible evidence, strong coverage/comparison, no material unresolved contradiction;
- **medium** — useful evidence but important limitations remain;
- **low** — directional/pilot evidence only;
- **unknown** — insufficient evidence for a claim.

Confidence is displayed next to the decision, not hidden in a methodology appendix.

## 6. Decision matrix

| condition | minimum decision |
|---|---|
| unlawful operation / unacceptable critical risk / common-floor breach | STOP |
| promising public benefit but material fixable weakness | MODIFY |
| safe bounded experiment still needs evidence | CONTINUE |
| predeclared public-value target met, floors pass, evidence adequate, ownership/exit ready | SCALE |
| target met technically but fairness/privacy/safety floor fails | STOP or MODIFY, never SCALE |
| strong Responsible STOP evidence | STOP + learning credit, not adaptation credit |

## 7. Net Public Value Record

Do **not** hide governance behind one opaque weighted score. The decision record should contain:

- primary public outcome;
- each common floor state: `pass / concern / fail / unknown`;
- subgroup/worst-case results;
- cost/dependency finding;
- incidents/complaints/override finding;
- confidence;
- reviewer dissent/uncertainty;
- final human decision and rationale.

An optional scenario-specific composite may support analysis only if weights are predeclared and raw components remain visible.

## 8. SCALE threshold discipline

No universal numeric threshold is asserted here. Integration/operations should define scenario targets before testing. As a governance default, SCALE normally requires:

- primary public-value target achieved;
- no critical floor failure;
- no unresolved critical `unknown`;
- at least medium evidence confidence for the primary outcome;
- tested operational fallback and maintenance owner;
- transfer/revalidation rule acknowledged.

## 9. Learning Unit interpretation

The same floors apply in all five areas; scenario metrics differ by local role. This prevents each district from inventing a convenient definition of success while still respecting different urban functions.
