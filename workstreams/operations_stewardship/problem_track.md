# Problem Track｜城市问题入口

> Proposed mechanism. City Agent/public-sector diagnosis may prepare evidence and options, but does not receive autonomous authority to create high-impact policy or deployments.

## 1. Principle

A Problem Track starts with an evidenced public problem, not “where can we use AI?”. The minimum chain is:

**diagnosis → challenge record → baseline → affected groups → public-value goal → non-AI alternatives → risk ceiling → bounded test → public review → STOP/MODIFY/CONTINUE/SCALE.**

## 2. Minimum challenge record

Required fields:
- `challenge_id`
- problem evidence and source/provenance
- affected groups and distributional hypothesis
- public-value goal
- baseline and counterfactual / comparison method
- non-AI alternatives considered
- why a technology intervention may be warranted
- data availability/status: Official / Verified / Derived / Assumed / Unknown
- operational risk ceiling
- proposed test site/time/window, explicitly Proposed if not official
- evaluation metrics and common safeguards
- budget/resources assumption, never represented as committed funding without evidence
- public feedback/objection/appeal route
- accountable owner placeholder/confirmed owner status
- maintenance and service-continuity requirement
- sunset / stop conditions

## 3. Intake gates

### Gate P0 — evidence of a real problem
Reject or return for evidence if the challenge is only an innovation slogan, vendor pitch or technology target. Evidence can include public operational statistics, public observations, verified service records, accessibility audits, environmental data or cleared community evidence appropriate to the claim.

### Gate P1 — public-value framing
Write a measurable public outcome. Examples of outcome form: reduce heat exposure at specified public-space conditions; improve wayfinding success for defined users; reduce time to access a human service; reduce unsafe conflicts in a bounded mobility setting. Do not substitute “number of AI deployments”.

### Gate P2 — non-AI option test
List at least one credible non-AI or simpler-service alternative. If the simpler alternative plausibly achieves the public value with lower risk/cost/maintenance burden, the correct result may be **STOP AI exploration / pursue non-AI intervention**.

### Gate P3 — risk ceiling and rights
Before soliciting solutions, declare the maximum tolerated impact and non-negotiable rights/safeguards. A bidder may propose a lower-risk solution; it may not exceed the public challenge's risk ceiling without reopening authorization.

### Gate P4 — testability and reversibility
A valid challenge must have a bounded way to learn. Define population/site/time boundaries, fallback service, human override where relevant, exit path and evidence sufficient to decide among STOP/MODIFY/CONTINUE/SCALE.

### Gate P5 — public legibility
Publish a plain-language challenge brief: what is wrong, what is known/unknown, who may be affected, what data may be used, what will not be automated, how to object/report problems and when results will be reviewed.

## 4. Challenge lifecycle

1. **Observed** — signal exists but evidence incomplete.
2. **Qualified** — baseline, affected groups and public-value goal documented.
3. **Open Challenge** — solution-neutral brief published with risk ceiling.
4. **Solutions Under Review** — AI and non-AI proposals compared.
5. **Authorized Trial** — one or more bounded interventions admitted.
6. **Evaluating** — evidence collected with complaints/incidents and distributional results.
7. **Decision** — STOP / MODIFY / CONTINUE / SCALE.
8. **Resolved / Reframed / Unresolved** — a problem can remain unresolved even if a technology works technically.

## 5. Evidence rules

- Never convert Assumed data into an existing-condition fact.
- Do not use provisional geometry as an official redline.
- Keep affected-group evidence separate from citywide averages.
- Record complaints, human takeover, incidents, exclusion/accessibility failures and unintended costs as evaluation data.
- Preserve disagreement when sources or reviewers conflict.
- A challenge can be closed because evidence shows the initial problem statement was wrong.

## 6. Public participation by risk

- Lower-impact challenge: open comments, accessible field observation, operator feedback, public result note.
- Moderate-impact challenge: targeted affected-group sessions, accessibility review, pre-trial notice, observer option, formal complaint route.
- Higher-impact challenge: representative affected-group review, explicit objection path, enhanced human responsibility/appeal, independent/professional review and public rationale before trial.

These are proposed operating expectations, not claims about statutory hearing requirements.

## 7. Anti-gaming

A challenge owner must not redefine the public-value target after seeing results merely to declare success. Material changes to baseline, metrics, target population or test scope are versioned and explained. Deployment count, model accuracy and publicity reach cannot substitute for the public-value objective.

## 8. Relationship to Urban Adaptation Rate

The Problem Track supplies the denominator and traceable lifecycle for Urban Adaptation Rate: diagnosed/qualified problems can be followed through trial and final public-value outcome. Responsible STOPs remain visible as learning; they should not be hidden merely because they do not become scaled deployments.
