# 5 / 10 / 20 Years｜长期演进路线

> Proposed roadmap. It describes institutional design goals, not committed government programs, budgets or construction schedules.

## North Star

The long-term asset is **Urban Capability Exchange Network｜城市能力交换网络**: a durable public mechanism for discovering, authorizing, testing, exchanging, evaluating, upgrading and retiring city capabilities even when today's models, vendors and hardware are obsolete.

## Year 0–5 — Institutionalize the learning loop

### Governance
- establish the Public Stewardship responsibility model with identifiable accountable public owner(s);
- operate Problem Track + Capability Track with common risk/public-value gates;
- make STOP/MODIFY/CONTINUE/SCALE decision records routine;
- establish community objection/appeal/human-alternative interfaces appropriate to risk;
- create standing professional/independent review capacity without outsourcing final accountability.

### Technical/operational durability
- establish Capability Registry and stable capability IDs;
- require maintenance owner, dependency inventory, rollback and sunset fields;
- standardize capability-level version/compatibility metadata;
- test manual/degraded service continuity;
- separate lifecycle maintenance planning from pilot launch funding.

### Public memory
- publish Annual City Version Releases;
- launch Retired Capability Archive governance and selected physical memory interface;
- connect Landmark as Memory to real version/decision history rather than promotional milestones.

### 5-year stress test
The system should survive the loss/replacement of a major vendor/model used by several capabilities without losing public evidence, accountability or essential-service continuity.

## Year 5–10 — Become an exchange network, not isolated pilots

### Cross-area revalidation
- capabilities migrate between Learning Units only after context revalidation;
- transfer packages include public-value evidence, risk assumptions, compatibility and maintenance information;
- local differences remain visible rather than being flattened into one citywide default.

### Interoperability and procurement maturity
- procurement describes public capability outcomes and exitability rather than a single permanent technical stack;
- conduct periodic migration/replacement exercises for critical dependencies;
- maintain technology-neutral capability records across model generations;
- build reusable public components and evaluation methods where licensing/rights permit.

### Institutional continuity
- preserve annual version and archive procedures through leadership/contract cycles;
- maintain community participation as standing infrastructure rather than project consultation;
- develop succession and knowledge-transfer rules for maintainers/reviewers.

### 10-year stress test
Select mature capabilities and replace core model/vendor/robotic components. The public-facing purpose, rights, evidence history and capability ID should remain intelligible while the technology changes.

## Year 10–20 — Make stewardship technology-agnostic

### Public infrastructure layer
The Capability Backbone matures into a network where discovery, authorization, public-value evidence, portability, maintenance, challenge and retirement are more stable than any particular AI architecture.

### Unknown future technology compatibility
Future systems may not resemble today's LLMs, robots, computer vision or apps. The governance contract should therefore ask technology-neutral questions:
- what public capability/problem relationship exists?
- who is affected?
- what authority and risk exist?
- what evidence proves public value?
- can operation be challenged/overridden/ended?
- who maintains it?
- how can it be replaced?
- what history must remain understandable?

### Memory continuity
Twenty years of City Version Releases and retirement records become a public learning archive showing not just technological progress but reversals, failed ideas, changing social priorities and responsible exits.

### Institutional anti-lock-in
No single model supplier, cloud platform, interface paradigm, annual event brand or visualization should be a critical dependency for governance continuity.

### 20-year stress test
Assume today's AI stack is unusable. If the city can still admit a new technology through the same public-value/risk/rights process, transfer it across contexts, evaluate it, publish a version decision and retire its predecessor safely, the Urban Capability Exchange Network has succeeded.

## Long-term invariants vs replaceable layers

| Keep stable | Intentionally replaceable |
|---|---|
| public accountability | models |
| public-value definition/process | vendors/clouds |
| risk-based authorization | sensors/robots/devices |
| affected-group rights/contestability | user interfaces |
| evidence provenance and uncertainty | specific metrics implementation |
| capability identity/history | software frameworks/APIs |
| maintenance/exit obligation | technical components |
| annual version memory | event formats/branding |
| retirement discipline | individual services |

## What must remain Unknown until later

Actual institution names, legal authority, budgets, staffing, procurement vehicles, technical standards, retention periods, risk thresholds and long-term capital plans cannot be responsibly fixed by this Agent. The roadmap instead defines interfaces that future competent institutions can fill without breaking the City OS logic.
