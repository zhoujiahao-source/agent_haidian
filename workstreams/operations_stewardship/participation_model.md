# Participation Model｜超越问卷的持续共治

## 1. Participation ladder

The participation system should match the decision and risk, rather than applying one generic survey to every issue.

| Mode | Purpose | Example output | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observe | understand actual conditions | field notes, accessibility walk, service journey | problem discovery / baseline |
| Inform | make capability legible | plain-language record, physical sign, registry page | all visible public capabilities |
| Listen | collect diverse experience | interviews, open comments, operator logs | low/moderate impact |
| Co-design | change scope/interface/evaluation | workshop prototype, success criteria, fallback design | moderate/high impact or contested services |
| Deliberate | compare trade-offs | citizen/affected-group panel recommendation, minority note | material public-value/risk trade-offs |
| Observe trial | inspect behavior in context | community observer report | novel or higher-impact trial |
| Object / appeal | challenge operation/outcome | case record, remedy, pause/review | rights-sensitive or materially harmful outcomes |
| Co-evaluate | judge evidence | evaluation panel, distributional review | before SCALE / major MODIFY |
| Set priorities | influence next challenge cycle | ranked unresolved problems with rationale | annual challenge setting |

## 2. Standing participation channels

Proposed year-round channels:
- public Problem Track submissions;
- community walkshops and accessibility audits;
- scenario labs at Learning Units;
- affected-group panels for higher-risk trials;
- open maintenance/incident clinics for scaled capabilities;
- public observer pool;
- appeal/objection desk with accessible non-digital route;
- annual unresolved-problem agenda setting;
- City Learning Assembly hearings tied to the version release.

## 3. Representative participation

Do not equate the loudest or most digitally active participants with the whole community. For higher-impact work, actively identify underrepresented affected groups and document who could not be reached. Representation strategy, compensation, safeguarding and recruitment methods require competent local implementation and are therefore Unknown, not invented here.

## 4. Evidence from participation

Participation produces structured evidence, not automatic truth. Each material input should record:
- issue/capability/challenge ID;
- participant role or affected-group relationship at a non-identifying level;
- method/date/context;
- main finding;
- strength/limitation;
- requested change;
- operator response;
- decision influence: adopted / partly adopted / not adopted / pending;
- rationale and evidence link.

This makes “we consulted” auditable.

## 5. Decision feedback loop

Every material participation round closes with a **You said / We checked / We changed / We did not change / Why / What remains open** record. High-quality disagreement may remain visible as a minority view rather than being erased by consensus language.

## 6. Relationship to annual versioning

Participation is continuous. The annual assembly consolidates, rather than replaces, year-round involvement. Release notes should identify major changes driven by community evidence, unresolved objections, distributional concerns and the next-year challenge priorities.

## 7. Anti-capture safeguards

- separate product promotion from public deliberation;
- disclose contributor conflicts where relevant;
- avoid participation metrics based only on number of attendees/comments;
- publish response-to-feedback rather than cherry-picked quotes;
- give non-digital/accessibility routes equal status;
- prevent a single vendor or highly resourced group from becoming the default definition of public need;
- retain public institution accountability even when community or expert panels advise strongly.
