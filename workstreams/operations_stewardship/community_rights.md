# Community Rights｜社区权利与可争议性

## 1. Position

Community participation is a rights-and-governance interface, not a satisfaction survey. The project should make it possible for affected people to understand, challenge, alter or exit a capability when appropriate, while preserving access to essential public service.

The mechanisms below are Proposed and must later be mapped to applicable law and actual competent institutions.

## 2. Minimum rights interface

### Right to know
People should be able to discover, in plain language, when a meaningful AI/algorithmic capability is operating, its purpose, accountable owner, relevant data categories, main limitations, human fallback and complaint route. Ambient low-risk infrastructure may use proportionate signage/registry disclosure rather than forcing constant consent dialogs.

### Right to a human route
Where a capability materially affects access, recommendation, eligibility, safety or individualized service, provide a realistic human/manual route appropriate to the service. Human override must be operational, not merely written in a policy.

### Right to object and report harm
Affected people should be able to report safety, privacy, accessibility, bias, nuisance, service exclusion and other public-value failures. Higher-risk trials should expose an explicit objection channel before and during operation.

### Right to contest material outcomes
Where an automated or AI-assisted output materially affects a person, the operating model should include a human-governed review/appeal route appropriate to the context. Exact statutory rights and deadlines remain Unknown here.

### Right to accessible alternatives
Public interfaces should account for disability, language, age, device access and digital literacy. A person should not need a smartphone, account or AI literacy merely to access a baseline public service unless a competent policy specifically and lawfully requires it.

### Right to collective representation
Some harms are collective rather than individual. Community organizations, accessibility representatives, worker representatives where applicable, caregivers and other affected-group delegates should be able to surface distributional patterns that isolated complaints miss.

## 3. Vulnerable-group review

For moderate/high-impact trials, review at least:
- who is absent from average-user testing;
- whether mobility, sensory, cognitive, language or digital-access differences alter outcomes;
- whether frontline workers absorb hidden workload or safety risk;
- whether children/older adults or other contextually vulnerable groups face different exposure;
- whether opt-out shifts costs onto people with fewer alternatives;
- whether model/service failure creates unequal access to human help.

Do not infer sensitive attributes merely to satisfy a metric. Use lawful, minimal, context-appropriate evidence and qualitative review where granular data would create greater risk.

## 4. Community stop signals

A community report is not automatically a veto, and volume alone is not a validity test. However, the governance process must define signals that force human review, such as:
- credible safety incident;
- repeated accessibility/service-denial pattern;
- evidence of unauthorized data use;
- serious mismatch between public notice and actual behavior;
- inability to reach promised human fallback;
- materially disproportionate burden on an affected group;
- trial operating beyond approved scope/time/site;
- maintenance or incident-response owner unavailable.

Threshold values are intentionally not invented here. Competent operators must define them before trial.

## 5. Appeal / objection handling pattern

1. Acknowledge and classify the issue without requiring technical expertise from the reporter.
2. Route urgent safety issues to immediate containment.
3. Identify accountable public owner and capability record.
4. Preserve minimum necessary evidence.
5. Offer an accessible human contact/review path.
6. Assess individual and collective/distributional impact.
7. Decide remedy: explanation, correction, service alternative, scope change, pause, STOP, compensation/other remedy where applicable law provides it.
8. Publish a non-identifying pattern/lesson in the capability record when appropriate.
9. Include material unresolved grievances and governance changes in City Version Release.

## 6. Observer role

For higher-impact or novel public trials, a public/community observer mechanism can increase legitimacy. Observers may inspect procedures, attend review sessions, verify that stated fallback/stop mechanisms exist and submit a minority note. They do not operate the system or assume public accountability.

## 7. Public-space interface

The Public Capability Interface should communicate status without turning the city into a wall of warnings:
- Ambient Layer: clear but low-friction disclosure for low-risk environmental capability;
- Consent Layer: affirmative interaction when personal/identifying/recommendation/personalized decisions are involved;
- Human Override: visible route to staff/manual operation;
- capability ID / QR or non-digital lookup path where practical;
- operational status and maintenance/incident notice for affected services.

## 8. No participation theater

A process fails the participation test if residents can comment but cannot discover what changed; if objections have no accountable recipient; if vulnerable-group review happens only after launch; if all metrics are averages; or if the only visible output is a celebratory annual event. Participation is proven by traceable influence on scope, safeguards, STOP/MODIFY decisions, priorities and release notes.
