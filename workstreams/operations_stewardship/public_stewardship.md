# Public Stewardship｜公共治理运营模型

> Status: **Proposed governance architecture**. It does not identify a real competent authority or claim adoption by any institution.

## 1. Purpose

The City OS is not a procurement catalogue of AI products. Its durable public function is to convert real urban problems into tested, reviewable and replaceable **Capabilities** while keeping accountability legible. The governance invariant is:

**public value first → risk-based authorization → reversible trial → evidence → STOP / MODIFY / CONTINUE / SCALE → exchange / retirement → public memory.**

Public Stewardship therefore means that public institutions retain responsibility for rules, authorization, data governance, public-value judgment, incident accountability and final SCALE/STOP decisions. External experts, universities, developers and communities contribute evidence and challenge decisions, but they do not dissolve final public responsibility.

## 2. Proposed institutional interfaces

The names below are functional placeholders, not existing organizations.

### 2.1 Public Stewardship Office (PSO) — proposed accountable owner

Accountable for:
- rule-setting and risk taxonomy;
- authorization/de-authorization;
- data-governance minimums;
- public-value review and distributional impact review;
- incident escalation and public accountability;
- procurement/maintenance continuity;
- final SCALE/STOP/RETIRE decisions;
- maintenance of the Capability Registry and City Version Release record.

The PSO may delegate preparation and low-risk operations, but not final accountability for high-impact public decisions.

### 2.2 Professional Review Pool — proposed evidence reviewer

A rotating pool spanning planning, public-space design, accessibility, safety, data governance, security, ethics, service operations and domain experts. It:
- tests claims and failure modes;
- reviews evidence quality and reversibility;
- recommends risk class and safeguards;
- records dissent where experts disagree.

It is **responsible for review, not accountable for government action**.

### 2.3 Research & Evaluation Network — proposed independent evaluation interface

Universities/research teams may run study design, counterfactual evaluation, longitudinal impact work and audit candidates. Research access must follow the same data minimization and authorization rules as operations. Academic participation does not create a privileged bypass around community rights or public accountability.

### 2.4 Capability Maintainers — developers / enterprises / civic teams

Every accepted capability must have an identifiable maintainer responsible for:
- technical documentation and provenance;
- supported versions/dependencies;
- incident response contact;
- maintenance window and deprecation notice;
- rollback/exit support;
- migration/export where technically feasible;
- transparent IP/open-source/licensing status.

No capability enters SCALE status with an undefined maintenance owner.

### 2.5 Community & Residents — rights-holders and co-design participants

Residents, workers, visitors and affected groups are not merely “users.” Depending on impact, they have routes to:
- propose problems;
- co-design trials and success criteria;
- object before or during higher-risk trials;
- report harm or accessibility failure;
- request human/non-AI alternatives where appropriate;
- appeal material outcomes;
- participate in vulnerable-group/distributional reviews;
- observe public review and version-release sessions.

### 2.6 Independent / Public Observation

Independent reviewers, civic observers and public-interest organizations may inspect published records and challenge evidence. Their function is to strengthen transparency and contestability, not to become an unaccountable parallel decision maker.

## 3. Authorization ladder

This workstream intentionally does **not** claim a statutory local risk classification. The following is a proposed operational ladder to be mapped to competent legal rules later.

| Operational tier | Typical characteristics | Minimum path | Decision authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — low impact / reversible | environmental or informational assistance; no material individualized decision; easily reversible | owner check + documented baseline + public notice where visible | delegated public operator under PSO rules |
| B — moderate public impact | automated operation in shared space, recommendation affecting access/experience, meaningful operational dependency | professional review + data/risk assessment + bounded trial + complaint/override path | PSO-designated public authority |
| C — high impact / rights-sensitive | personal identification, high-impact recommendation/decision, essential-service effect, significant physical/safety risk | enhanced professional review + affected-group participation + explicit human responsibility + appeal + strict rollback/stop plan | competent public authority; no vendor self-authorization |
| Prohibited / not ready | unacceptable risk, unlawful/uncleared data, irreversibility without justification, no human responsibility, no viable exit | no trial | STOP |

Risk authorization is a **ceiling**, not a reward. Technical sophistication never creates greater authority.

## 4. Public-value gate

Every capability must answer:
1. What real problem is evidenced?
2. Who benefits, and who may bear risk/cost?
3. What is the non-AI alternative?
4. Why is technology necessary or materially better?
5. What baseline will be compared?
6. What safety/fairness/privacy/environment/cost floor must never be violated?
7. What scenario-specific benefit metric is expected?
8. What evidence would trigger STOP, MODIFY, CONTINUE or SCALE?
9. How can people contest, opt out or reach a human where relevant?
10. What is the sunset condition and who executes retirement?

A technically successful pilot can still be a public-value failure. A timely STOP can be a successful learning outcome.

## 5. Decision states and required records

### STOP
Use when minimum safeguards fail, harms exceed tolerance, the need disappears, the non-AI alternative is superior, maintenance collapses, evidence remains inadequate, or legitimacy/authorization is absent. Record reason, affected groups, cleanup, replacement and learning.

### MODIFY
Keep the problem open but change capability, scope, data, operating hours, interface, safeguards, maintainer or evaluation design. Reauthorization is required if risk materially changes.

### CONTINUE
Continue the bounded trial because minimum safeguards hold and evidence is promising but insufficient for scale. Publish what remains unknown.

### SCALE
Scale only when evidence shows public-value improvement, distributional harms are acceptable/mitigated, operational ownership is durable, interoperability/exit are credible and competent human authorization is explicit.

## 6. Accountability chain for incidents

1. Make the situation safe; enable human/manual service and emergency stop where relevant.
2. Preserve minimum necessary incident evidence without expanding surveillance.
3. Notify the accountable public owner and maintainer.
4. Suspend or constrain the capability when the pre-declared stop rule is met.
5. Assess affected groups, including accessibility/distributional effects.
6. Provide an appropriate complaint/appeal route and public-facing incident summary when disclosure is lawful/safe.
7. Decide STOP/MODIFY/CONTINUE through the appropriate authorization tier.
8. Feed the incident and corrective action into the next City Version Release and, if retired, the Retired Capability Archive.

## 7. Procurement and maintenance principles

- Buy **outcomes + exitability**, not permanent dependency on one model/vendor.
- Require asset/data/configuration inventories appropriate to the capability.
- Require documented interfaces and export/migration paths where feasible.
- Separate pilot funding from lifecycle maintenance funding.
- Require deprecation/termination support and credential/data cleanup.
- Avoid incentives based on deployment counts. Reward verified public-value improvement, responsible STOPs, maintenance quality and successful migration/retirement.

## 8. Relationship to spatial concept

Public Stewardship is the institutional layer of the **Capability Backbone**. The five learning areas can originate, test and revalidate capabilities, but the registry, risk rules, public-value evidence, rights and retirement interface make the capabilities exchangeable rather than tied to one park, vendor or technology generation.

## 9. Locked long-term invariant

Twenty years from now, today's models, robots and interfaces may be obsolete. The public institution must still be able to answer: **what problem was addressed, who authorized it, what evidence existed, who was affected, how it could be challenged, whether it delivered public value, how it was changed or retired, and what replaced it.**
