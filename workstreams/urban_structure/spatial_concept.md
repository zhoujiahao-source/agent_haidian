# Spatial Concept — City OS as Urban Space

## 1. One-sentence spatial concept

**The Jing-Zhang Capability Backbone is not a single AI boulevard; it is a continuous public-realm armature where movement, heritage memory, blue-green systems, daily services, capability interfaces and reversible experiments overlap, allowing the Three Areas and Two Wings to exchange and revalidate public capabilities.**

This is a conceptual urban-design proposition, not an approved plan or engineering alignment.

## 2. From abstract City OS to drawable urban design

The locked City OS logic is translated into five spatial operations:

1. **Make the backbone physically reachable.** Use existing / future-verified public routes, park paths and crossings as the base. Do not define continuity by a line on a diagram.
2. **Differentiate learning units.** Each of the Three Areas / Two Wings gets a primary capability, supporting capabilities, a distinct public-space/building prototype, and an explicit exchange relationship with the backbone.
3. **Turn public capability into an interface.** AI is background infrastructure; public space contains simple interfaces for navigation, cooling, accessibility, community help, emergency response, cultural interpretation and other low-risk capabilities, with consent and human override where personal data or individualized decisions are involved.
4. **Make experimentation reversible.** Permanent infrastructure carries access, shade, safety, drainage and universal design; adaptive components change by season/program; experimental components are removable, time-limited and evaluated through STOP / MODIFY / CONTINUE / SCALE.
5. **Store learning in place.** Landmark nodes record successes, modifications, failures and retirements, linking the 1909 railway memory to the 2026 city-learning archive and future experiments.

## 3. Spatial grammar

### 3.1 Backbone + Learning Units

The plan should be read as two interacting systems:

- **Capability Backbone:** continuous / near-continuous public-space and movement structure plus information/operation layer.
- **Learning Units:** district-scale areas with different capacities for producing, testing, receiving and evaluating capabilities.

The backbone is not a centralizing “engine”. Any learning unit can initiate a problem or capability, then use the network to find a different place for testing, feedback, professional support or market validation.

### 3.2 Longitudinal sequence

North-south continuity should be represented as a sequence of urban conditions rather than one homogeneous greenway. Candidate segment archetypes:

1. **Research / Verification Segment** — porous research edge, shared test courtyards, developer exchange, controlled L2/L3 test spaces.
2. **Neighborhood Learning Segment** — daily community services, talent living, analog alternatives, consent-based interfaces, small civic rooms.
3. **Exchange / Memory Segment** — heritage interpretation, annual city-version display, retired-capability archive, public review space.
4. **Market / Night Segment** — transit-linked commerce, AI-native service prototypes, culture/night uses and service-recovery desks.
5. **Blue-Green Test Segment** — park/water-edge public life, low-risk ambient services, climate adaptation tests and low-sensing refuge.

Exact segment boundaries are intentionally not fixed until official geometry and verified existing conditions are available.

### 3.3 Cross-stitches

East-west stitching is a hierarchy, not a repeated decorative line:

- **Structural Stitch:** verified existing or feasible public crossing that can carry universal-access movement and essential services.
- **Public-Space Stitch:** a sequence of plazas, ground-floor passages, pocket spaces, crossings and wayfinding that makes a real route understandable and comfortable.
- **Experimental Stitch:** temporary / reversible interventions that test demand or operating patterns without pretending to solve a structural barrier.

The existing `ROAD-002`–`ROAD-006` lines are retained only as candidate study axes; none is promoted to a confirmed crossing in this workstream.

## 4. Public-space-first rules

1. AI facilities do not replace basic urban quality. Shade, seating, drainage, accessibility, lighting, safe crossings, active frontages and clear wayfinding are prerequisites.
2. Every high-tech interface must have a low-tech or human alternative when failure would exclude a user.
3. Low-risk environmental capabilities may be ambient; recognition, recommendation, personalization or high-impact decisions require active consent and visible human responsibility.
4. Sensor-light / analog refuge spaces are part of the network, not leftovers.
5. Public space should host ordinary life all day: commuting, exercise, caregiving, eating, meeting, waiting, play, cultural use and night activity.

## 5. Drawing rules for Final Integration

The final plan should distinguish four graphic statuses simultaneously:

- **Known:** verified existing features.
- **Estimated:** derived existing condition with method and confidence.
- **Proposed:** this design's spatial action.
- **Unknown:** data gap that must not be filled by graphic invention.

Provisional boundaries should be thin, dashed, low-contrast and explicitly labeled. The visual hierarchy should instead emphasize the capability exchange network, nodes, section callouts, stitch hierarchy and permanent/adaptive/experimental layer.

## 6. Phase 1 conclusion

The Capability Backbone should be drawn as a **layered public armature plus node network**, not a single colored corridor. Its urban-design test is simple: it must be visible in plan, section, daily use, operations and phasing — not only in a system diagram.
