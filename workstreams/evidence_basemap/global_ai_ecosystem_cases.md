# Global AI / Urban Innovation Ecosystem Cases

Purpose: evidence precedents for `agent.2`, with a strict separation between **case fact** and **Jing-Zhang transfer hypothesis**. These are background precedents, not local statutory evidence.

## 1. Punggol Digital District — Singapore

**Primary evidence:** JTC Singapore; Singapore Institute of Technology/JTC releases.  
**Verified facts:** PDD is a 50-hectare district integrating a business park with Singapore Institute of Technology. JTC describes the Open Digital Platform as a district operating layer using real-time data; current materials describe industry-academia-government collaboration and the district as a real-world/living test environment.  
**Spatial/institutional mechanism:** colocated university + firms + shared district data/operations platform + precinct-scale testbed.  
**Known value:** lowers coordination barriers between applied research, urban operations and industry pilots; current JTC materials show real test partners using the platform.  
**Limitation:** Singapore’s land/institutional/data governance context differs substantially from Beijing; sensor coverage, governance and investment scale cannot be copied.  
**Transfer to Jing-Zhang:** a strong precedent for **Capability Backbone** as an interoperability/testing interface, provided public-data minimization, authorization and human accountability are stronger and explicit.

## 2. Seoul Digital Inclusion — Seoul, Republic of Korea

**Primary evidence:** Seoul Metropolitan Government.  
**Verified facts:** the city’s Digital Inclusion program uses neighborhood learning environments, one-to-one tutoring, help desks, senior-focused digital literacy, device-experience spaces and educational robots/AI tutor pilots.  
**Spatial/institutional mechanism:** citywide digital services are paired with neighborhood physical support and human assistance.  
**Known value:** demonstrates that digital-city capability requires a non-digital/human support layer, especially for seniors and digitally vulnerable groups.  
**Limitation:** published program metrics span different historic years and should not be treated as current universal performance.  
**Transfer to Jing-Zhang:** every Public Capability Interface should have a low-barrier human/physical fallback; inclusion is infrastructure, not a UI add-on.

## 3. Toyota Woven City — Susono, Japan

**Primary evidence:** Toyota Woven City official site and co-creation materials.  
**Verified facts:** Woven City explicitly calls itself a “test course for mobility,” where inventors co-create, test and refine products/services in city-like daily-life conditions with residents/visitors (“Weavers”) providing feedback; digital-twin and full-scale testing resources are offered to selected inventors.  
**Spatial/institutional mechanism:** controlled living laboratory + physical/digital test course + feedback loop + inventor support.  
**Known value:** clear experiment → feedback → refinement cycle embedded in a lived environment.  
**Limitation:** it is a corporate-controlled test environment, not an ordinary public city. Default access, consent, risk authority and accountability cannot be imported unchanged.  
**Transfer to Jing-Zhang:** useful for reversible pilot infrastructure and explicit experiment stages, but Jing-Zhang must add public-interest authorization, opt-out, independent review, STOP/MODIFY/CONTINUE/SCALE and Human Override.

## 4. Decidim Barcelona — Barcelona, Spain

**Primary evidence:** Barcelona City Council.  
**Verified facts:** Decidim.Barcelona is an open-source citizen participation platform supporting debate, proposals, comments/support and proposal tracking across digital and face-to-face processes.  
**Spatial/institutional mechanism:** traceable civic decision interface rather than hidden administrative feedback.  
**Known value:** shows how proposals and their evolution can remain inspectable rather than disappearing after consultation.  
**Limitation:** participation traceability does not grant AI systems authority to decide; platform participation is not equivalent to representative consent for high-impact automation.  
**Transfer to Jing-Zhang:** useful precedent for transparent experiment registers, public feedback, change histories and Retired Capability Archive records.

## 5. Kalasatama — Helsinki, Finland

**Primary evidence:** City of Helsinki.  
**Verified facts:** Kalasatama is a long-term redevelopment of a former harbour/industrial area into a dense mixed-use waterfront district, with public transport, schools, day care, health/well-being services and a planned six-kilometre coastal route. Helsinki has also documented smart-city pilots and transfer of tested methods to other districts.  
**Spatial/institutional mechanism:** smart-city experimentation is embedded in an ordinary mixed-use district with everyday services, not isolated in an exhibition park.  
**Known value:** supports the principle that innovation should live within transit, housing, public realm and daily services.  
**Limitation:** land-use intensity, development finance and local plan controls are Helsinki-specific and cannot be copied.  
**Transfer to Jing-Zhang:** prioritize everyday public-life continuity and the ability to move successful capabilities from one learning unit to another.

## 6. Enabling Village — Singapore

**Primary evidence:** SG Enable.  
**Verified facts:** Enabling Village is Singapore’s first inclusive community space, bringing together community amenities, retail, inclusive programming, disability-focused training/employment and an assistive-technology centre; SG Enable emphasizes universal design, barrier-free access and assistive technology.  
**Spatial/institutional mechanism:** accessibility is built into physical space, service operations and technology support together.  
**Known value:** demonstrates a combined built-environment + service + assistive-technology model rather than treating accessibility as signage compliance.  
**Limitation:** it is a specialized national inclusion hub, not a city-scale AI district governance model.  
**Transfer to Jing-Zhang:** Public Capability Interfaces and Ambient/Consent layers should be audited for sensory, mobility and cognitive accessibility; human alternatives must remain visible and usable.

## Cross-case synthesis for Jing-Zhang

| transferable mechanism | strongest precedent | evidence-layer recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| shared interoperability/test layer | Punggol | define interface fields and provenance; do not prescribe proprietary platform |
| lived feedback + iterative testing | Woven City | pair with public authorization and reversibility |
| human fallback / digital inclusion | Seoul + Enabling Village | map service-support nodes and accessibility gaps |
| traceable proposal/change history | Decidim | maintain experiment/change/retirement records |
| everyday mixed-use embedding | Kalasatama | evaluate access, transit, public-space and service continuity |

**Evidence conclusion:** no single global case should become the master model. The transferable pattern is a **portfolio**: interoperable testing + real-world feedback + inclusive human support + traceable governance + ordinary urban life. This aligns with the locked City OS / Capability Backbone / Public Value logic without importing foreign quantitative controls.
