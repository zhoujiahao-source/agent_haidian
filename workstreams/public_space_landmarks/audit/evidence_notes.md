# Evidence Notes — Public Space & Landmarks

Retrieved: 2026-08-08

These notes distinguish factual background from design proposals. External sources below are public Beijing-government sources; they do not provide this workstream with parcel-level ownership, statutory control, exact heritage GIS or approval to implement the proposed landmarks.

## E-01 — Jing-Zhang Railway, 1909

**Publisher:** Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau / Beijing historic-city-protection public material.

**Supported fact:** public Beijing sources state that the Beijing–Zhangjiakou Railway was completed/opened in 1909 and describe its importance as a railway independently designed/built by Chinese engineers.

**Use in this workstream:** supports `1909` as a high-confidence historical time anchor and the narrative of engineering capability.

**Does not support:** exact project-site heritage boundary, ownership, permitted intervention or authenticity of any particular object in the Haidian corridor.

Public references:
- https://wwj.beijing.gov.cn/bjww/362760/362770/743594241/index.html
- https://ghzrzyw.beijing.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/tzgg/sj/202207/P020220905553447226470.pdf

## E-02 — Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park Phase I

**Publisher:** Beijing Municipal Forestry and Parks Bureau.

**Supported fact:** official information states Phase I was fully completed/opened in 2023, from Qinghuadong Road to Zhichun Road, about 2.5 km / 16.8 ha, with preservation/recovery of railway traces, old rails/switches/rolling-stock elements and Qinghuayuan station; the public-space project also aimed to stitch previously divided space and provide everyday public services.

**Use in this workstream:** supports the public-space/heritage-memory premise and the importance of east–west stitching and ordinary daily use.

**Does not support:** treating these dimensions as the boundary of the current 11.4 km² design scope or placing a proposed landmark on a specific parcel.

Reference:
- https://yllhj.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk/zwxx/202306/t20230626_3145467.shtml

## E-03 — Park planning logic: public space + east–west stitching

**Publisher:** Beijing Municipal Commission of Planning and Natural Resources.

**Supported fact:** 2021 planning interpretation described a roughly 9 km heritage-park concept from Beijing North Station to the North Fifth Ring, with a goal of using the released ground corridor to improve public space and repair east–west connections.

**Use in this workstream:** supports the conceptual need to test both north–south continuity and east–west stitching without inventing bridges/tunnels/redlines.

Reference:
- https://ghzrzyw.beijing.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/zxzt/wsghs/2021dej/d5j/202112/t20211216_2562970.html

## E-04 — Latest observed Phase II public-space update

**Publisher:** Beijing Municipal Forestry and Parks Bureau.

**Published:** 2026-07-13.

**Supported fact:** official information states the supporting works for Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Public Space Phase II were completed; the north segment extends from Qinghuadong Road toward the North Fifth Ring, and the project describes a north–south / east–west “fishbone” slow-mobility network connecting walking, running, cycling, children's activity, plant education and exercise/community functions.

**Use in this workstream:** confirms that, as of the start date of this Agent work, north–south continuity, east–west linkage and mixed daily-life public-space functions remain highly relevant to current real-world implementation context.

**Does not support:** claiming this Agent's nodes, landmarks, AI systems or test programs are part of the built Phase II project.

Reference:
- https://yllhj.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk/zwxx/202607/t20260714_4761226.shtml

## Evidence confidence summary

- **High:** 1909 anchor; 2023 Phase I public-space facts; 2026 Phase II public update; repository rule/provisional status.
- **Medium:** using these facts to justify the *type* of public-space/heritage design response.
- **Low / Proposed:** exact future node location, size, operating model, staffing, trial technology, utilities, implementation timing or cost.

## Conflict / freshness note

This workstream uses the later 2023 full-completion figure when a summary is necessary and does not convert either value into a statutory design boundary.
