# Sections & Experience Sequences｜剖面与体验序列

## 1. Drawing principle

These are **programmatic sections**, not engineering drawings. They define relationships, fallback layers and user experience. Exact widths, slopes, structural systems, utilities, fire access and construction details must be set by verified survey/standards/professional design.

Across all sections, the order is:

**public-space baseline in foreground → optional capability interface at the edge → service/operations layer backstage → data/AI infrastructure least visually dominant.**

## 2. Section A — Climate Porch / 高温公共空间剖面

### Foreground: ordinary civic life
- continuous accessible walking surface;
- verified safe crossing/edge conditions;
- shade from trees/canopy as primary thermal strategy;
- seating with backs/arms and resting choices;
- drinking water where feasible and approved;
- planting, soil/permeability and maintenance access.

### Interface edge
- small public status marker: heat condition / drinking-water or facility status / manual contact;
- optional route-help point that does not block circulation;
- static information remains complete without a display.

### Backstage
- environmental sensors only as needed for operation/evaluation;
- approved active cooling equipment, if justified by measured benefit;
- maintenance isolation, drainage/water/electrical service subject to engineering design;
- operations staff can set MANUAL/OFF.

### Failure sequence
`forecast/control unavailable → active equipment manual/off → shade/seating/water/route remain usable`.

### Evidence to add later
Field thermal survey, shade mapping, tree/landscape condition, water/energy capacity, maintenance burden and user comfort by subgroup.

## 3. Section B — Care Crossing / 无障碍缝合剖面

### Foreground
- continuous step-free route designed from verified level/crossing conditions;
- tactile + visual + audible redundancy where appropriate;
- clear pedestrian priority/safe waiting according to professional traffic design;
- resting point outside the movement clear zone;
- ordinary wayfinding visible at decision points.

### Interface edge
- assistance contact and barrier-status notice;
- optional accessible-route request using the user's stated need;
- no camera-based inference that someone is disabled.

### Backstage
- verified elevator/crossing/service status feeds where public/available;
- human mobility/service team handles uncertain or high-consequence cases;
- barrier reports enter an accountable maintenance queue.

### Failure sequence
`live status unavailable → digital route marked degraded/off → physical accessible route + conventional cues + human assistance remain`.

### Critical review
A digital “accessible route” cannot compensate for a physically inaccessible crossing. Any such conflict is a **spatial blocker**, not a software inconvenience.

## 4. Section C — Reversible Test Yard / 可逆实验剖面

### Public side
- ordinary public route is continuous and independent;
- observation edge gives a clear view without requiring participation;
- trial card displays problem, status, risk class, test window, steward, stop contact and nonparticipant route.

### Controlled threshold
- physical/operational separation appropriate to the trial;
- entry only under the trial's participation/authorization rules;
- no ambiguity between everyday circulation and test space.

### Test bay
- removable/reconfigurable equipment and markings where feasible;
- minimum necessary sensing/data collection;
- visible safe-state indicators;
- qualified operator oversight.

### Backstage / reset
- maintenance/service route;
- manual stop/control;
- secure equipment/data handling;
- storage, reset, removal and safe disposal/reuse process.

### Failure sequence
`fault / boundary breach / stop condition → immediate safe state → controlled zone closes → public bypass remains → incident review → 2026 archive receives decision record`.

## 5. Section D — City Learning Archive / 城市学习档案剖面

### Public ground
- ordinary seating/passage and physical timeline;
- Annual City Version Release visible without login/app;
- STOP/MODIFY cases have equal spatial dignity with SCALE cases.

### Review layer
- evidence tables and public review/community table;
- paper/accessible feedback path;
- clear status/confidence/source labels.

### Curatorial/steward layer
- staff verify publication, attribution, corrections and privacy redaction;
- AI may help retrieve/summarize but does not issue the final public verdict.

### Protected record layer
- sensitive/raw evidence is not exposed in the public exhibit;
- retention/access follows the responsible institution's rules.

### Failure sequence
`digital archive unavailable → physical version record + staff/public meeting + static evidence cards continue`.

## 6. Sequence 01 — Commuter, ordinary weekday

1. Enters the north–south public-space sequence with conventional wayfinding.
2. Crosses a verified east–west seam through a Care Crossing.
3. Reads a small capability status only if useful; no app prompt blocks movement.
4. Chooses an optional route update during a disruption.
5. At night, follows continuous low-glare lighting and static transit direction.
6. If the digital service is off, the journey is still complete.

**Design test:** the Capability Backbone must feel first like good city fabric, not an interface tunnel.

## 7. Sequence 02 — Older resident in hot weather

1. Uses shaded walking/rest network without registration.
2. Climate Porch provides seating/water and a simple heat-status notice.
3. Resident may ask for a lower-exposure route; consent is limited to that request.
4. Community Service Port provides human assistance when a service question is unclear.
5. Active cooling failure changes the status to MANUAL/OFF; passive comfort remains.

**Design test:** no smartphone is required for essential comfort or service access.

## 8. Sequence 03 — Disabled user across the corridor

1. Plans/undertakes a physically continuous route verified by professional accessibility audit.
2. At a seam, tactile/visual/audio cues provide direct information.
3. Optional route assistance uses stated needs, never inferred identity.
4. A temporary barrier is reported; human operations confirm and repair/escalate it.
5. The barrier-resolution history can later become evidence in the 2026 learning archive.

**Design test:** route completion and detour burden are evaluated across different access needs.

## 9. Sequence 04 — Visitor through 1909 → 2026 → Future → Retired

### 1909 — Memory
Visitor encounters verified railway history through material ground, time/mileage marks and an everyday resting route. Optional multilingual interpretation adds depth.

### 2026 — Learning
The same line/node language becomes an archive of urban experiments: a capability was proposed, tested, modified/stopped/scaled. The visitor sees evidence and human responsibility, not only technology.

### Future — Unfinished
An unresolved public problem is shown beside a reversible test bay. The visitor can observe without participating; current authorization/status is explicit.

### Retired — Exit
A selected retired capability explains why it left service, what data was deleted/retained, what physical artifact happened to it and what replaced it.

**Narrative:** `BUILD CAPABILITY → LEARN PUBLIC VALUE → TEST THE UNRESOLVED → EXIT RESPONSIBLY`.

## 10. Sequence 05 — Developer/researcher test lifecycle

1. Brings a public problem/capability candidate to a Capability Interchange.
2. Risk/authorization review determines whether a real-world trial is appropriate.
3. Reversible Test Yard is configured with bypass, stop condition and evidence plan.
4. Trial runs only in the declared window.
5. Operators/public evidence goes to City Learning Archive.
6. Decision becomes STOP / MODIFY / CONTINUE / SCALE.
7. Cross-zone revalidation is required before claiming broad transferability.
8. Obsolete/failed/superseded capability is retired with a lesson record.

**Design test:** the physical system makes stopping/removal as normal as installation.

## 11. AI backstage diagram logic

For future figure production, show four stacked layers rather than a glowing AI core:

1. **People + public space** — primary, largest layer.
2. **Conventional infrastructure + staff** — visible support and accountable fallback.
3. **Capability interface** — small, optional/ambient depending risk.
4. **AI/data systems** — thin backstage layer connected by controlled arrows.

This visual hierarchy is a direct safeguard against turning the proposal into an AI exhibition.
