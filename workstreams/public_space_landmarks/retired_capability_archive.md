# Retired Capability Archive｜城市能力退役档案

## 1. Purpose

A learning city needs a dignified way to end technologies and services. The Retired Capability Archive preserves **public lessons**, not obsolete systems for nostalgia and not a “failure shame museum.”

It records why a capability existed, what value it created, what limitations or incidents emerged, why it left active service, what replaced it and what future designers should learn.

## 2. Retirement triggers

A capability enters retirement review when one or more conditions are met:

- public value is persistently below the declared threshold;
- safety risk or incident pattern cannot be acceptably mitigated;
- unequal impact remains material after modification;
- privacy/data burden exceeds justified public value;
- environmental/resource cost is disproportionate;
- operating/maintenance burden makes service unreliable;
- a simpler non-AI or newer capability performs the public task better;
- technical/vendor support ends;
- legal/standards/authorization conditions change;
- the original urban problem disappears or changes materially.

Retirement is a governance decision by responsible humans/institutions, not an automatic model decision.

## 3. Required archive label

Each public archive item uses the following template:

- **capability_name/version**
- **period_active**
- **problem_addressed**
- **public_value_evidence**
- **incidents/limitations**
- **reason_for_retirement**
- **replacement**
- **data_retention/deletion**
- **physical_artifact_status**
- **lessons_for_future**

Recommended additional fields:

- archive_id
- steward / responsible institution role
- prior risk class
- prior status path (`trial → service → modified → retired`, etc.)
- locations/context where it was tested or served
- affected groups / subgroup findings
- decision date + review method
- source/evidence references
- confidence / missing evidence
- rights/license constraints for displayed media or objects

## 4. Three archive levels

### Level A — Public lesson card

Default for most retired capabilities. A concise physical card in the 2026 City Learning Archive and/or distributed Memory Switch nodes. It explains the life cycle and reason for retirement without storing unnecessary hardware.

### Level B — Physical artifact sample

Used only when an object materially helps public understanding and can be stored/displayed safely and lawfully. Keep the minimum representative component, not whole fleets or hazardous/large equipment by default.

### Level C — Deep digital/research record

Detailed technical evidence may be retained by the responsible institution under applicable governance rules. The public exhibit links to an appropriate reviewed summary rather than exposing raw sensitive/security data.

The public landmark should therefore remain **physical-first but artifact-light**: memory is carried by place, labels, selected objects and accountable records, not warehouse accumulation.

## 5. Data retirement protocol

Retiring a capability triggers a specific data review:

1. identify what data is still legally/operationally required;
2. delete or de-identify data with no continuing justified purpose;
3. preserve only minimum evidence needed for accountability, audit or research under the relevant governance basis;
4. remove credentials, keys, personal accounts and operational secrets from display artifacts;
5. ensure public archive material does not allow re-identification of individuals;
6. record the retention/deletion decision on the archive label.

The archive must never use “historical interest” as an automatic justification for retaining personal data.

## 6. Physical artifact retirement protocol

Before public display/storage:

- isolate/remove power sources, fluids, sharp/moving parts or other hazards as professionally required;
- wipe storage media and credentials according to approved procedure;
- document whether the object is original, modified, deactivated, partial or reconstructed;
- respect ownership, IP, copyright and disposal obligations;
- prioritize reuse/recycling where safe and appropriate;
- do not imply an artifact was deployed citywide if it was only a concept or trial.

## 7. Spatial form

The archive is proposed as a **distributed memory layer** connected to the 2026 City Learning Archive rather than a single dark “cemetery” building.

Possible physical components:

- **Retirement Shelf:** selected small artifacts + life-cycle labels;
- **Version Track:** a linear record showing replacement/succession;
- **Stop Bench:** a calm reading/seating element around a major public lesson;
- **Reuse Workshop Window:** where safe components/materials can be prepared for reuse or education;
- **Distributed Archive Tags:** placed at former capability sites only when factual, useful and operationally appropriate.

Public-facing naming remains **Retired Capability Archive / 城市能力退役档案**. “Capability cemetery” may be an internal metaphor but should not frame the public experience.

## 8. Interpretation rules

Every retired item should answer five public questions:

1. What problem were we trying to solve?
2. What did this capability actually improve, if anything?
3. What went wrong or became outdated?
4. Why was retirement better than continuation?
5. What changed in the replacement or next experiment?

This produces a culture of responsible exit rather than fear of failure.

## 9. Relationship to Annual City Version Release

The annual version release includes a **Retired** section alongside Added / Modified / Scaled / Stopped / Unresolved. Significant retirements are then transferred into the archive with a stable reference so future teams can avoid repeating known problems.

## 10. Red-team checks

The archive fails if:

- it humiliates teams or affected communities;
- it sensationalizes accidents;
- it displays personal/sensitive data;
- it keeps hazardous or insecure hardware for spectacle;
- it treats every obsolete device as culturally valuable;
- it hides the public-value evidence behind technical jargon;
- it turns STOP into a negative score rather than a possible sign of responsible learning.

## Phase 8 checkpoint

- **Completed:** retirement criteria, public label, data deletion, physical artifact handling, distributed spatial form and interpretation logic.
- **Found:** responsible deletion and safe material exit are part of public memory, not back-office tasks.
- **Unknown:** statutory record-retention periods, archive operator, storage capacity, disposal contracts and artifact ownership.
- **Next dependency:** express the whole system through sections and user sequences that show AI backstage and public space continuing under failure.
